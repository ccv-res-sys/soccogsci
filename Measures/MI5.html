5D Measure of Mind Inference (MI5)

This measure, developed in Malle (2009) can be downloaded and used free of charge. However, if you collect any data with it (published or not), please share as much as you are willing to share (at least the MI5 rating matrix itself, so we can continue to test dimensionality).

    Malle B. F. (2019). How many dimensions of mind perception really are there? In A. K. Goel, C. M. Seifert, & C. Freksa (Eds.), Proceedings of the 41st Annual Meeting of the Cognitive Science Society (pp. 2268-2274). Montreal, QB: Cognitive Science Society.

Overview

Previous research suggests that people’s folk conception of the mind is organized along a few fundamental dimensions (e.g., D’Andrade, 1987; Gray, Gray, & Wegner, 2007; Weisman, Dweck, & Markman, 2017); but studies disagree on the exact number of those dimensions. Using an expanded item pool of mental capacities, variations of question probes, and numerous judged agents, we have conducted five studies that provide consistent evidence for three dimensions of perceived mind: Affect (A), Moral and Mental Regulation (M), and Reality Interaction (R). In addition we show that, under some conditions, two of the three dimensions further divide: A divides into negative and positive (social) affect, and M divides into moral cognition and social cognition.

We offer here the 20-item instrument that most clearly and reliably measures people’s 3-dimensional, and sometimes 5-dimensional, representations of human and other minds. For details of item selection and validation of the instrument's structure, see Malle (2019).

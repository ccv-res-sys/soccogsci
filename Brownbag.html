Social Cognitive Science Brownbag Series

 

 

In the Fall 2009, we began organizing meetings for faculty and graduate students in psychology to present current research projects. Since then, the meetings have grown from a small group of psychologists to include scholars from cognitive science, neuroscience, philosophy, political science, and religious studies. In the winter of 2010, the meeting series was named the Social Cognitive Brown Bag to reflect its focus on a broad, interdisciplinary study of social cognition.

Our meetings have three goals: (1) To provide an interdisciplinary forum for faculty and students interested in social cognitive science—in work ranging from group dynamics to computational social cognition, from language acquisition to judgment and decision making, from theory of mind to action control; (2) to create opportunities for students and faculty to discuss and receive feedback on ongoing and prospective research projects; and (3) to build an intellectual community in which students and faculty who share basic interests become more aware of the research conducted outside their immediate labs. This growing community extends beyond Brown, as we invite speakers from other institutions as well.

Meetings are held every other Friday in Hunter 168 from 12:00-1:30. Graduate students and faculty interested in presenting current research (including upcoming talks and posters or even manuscript drafts) should email Andrew underscore Monroe at Brown.edu.

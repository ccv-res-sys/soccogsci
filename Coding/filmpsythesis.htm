Honors Thesis on Film and Social Psychology

This is an opportunity for a sharp, creative, and self-motivated undergraduate student who has interests in both film/acting and psychology and, within psychology, is particularly fascinated by such topics as empathy, theory of mind, and perceptions of what makes a person “authentic.”

The project is inspired by a journalist’s observation made just before the 2007 Oscars — that actors appear to be more likely to be nominated for an Oscar if they play a real-life character (e.g., Forest Whitaker as Idi Amin, Helen Mirren as Queen Elizabeth) than if they play fictional characters.  The honors project will attempt to answer whether audiences really are more convinced by an actor’s acting ability if they believe that the actor plays a real-life character.  Moreover, if this is the case, the project would try to determine what drives this perception. Is it the ability to empathize with people whom one considers real? Or the ability to “fill in” assumed characteristics of real people but not fictional characters?

The experimental setup is fairly straightforward: Show people a variety of film clips in which an actor acts well.  For each clip, tell half of the people that the actor plays a real-life character and the other half that the actor plays a fictional character.Then measure people’s assessments of acting quality along with their empathy with the character, their liking of the character, their inferences about the character’s emotion expressions and mind states, and inferences about the character’s personality.

The film buff in you will enjoy identifying clips from lesser-known films so that the central manipulation is believable and successful.  The talented psychologist in you will plan and execute the experiment with attention to detail so as make likely that the results are meaningful and perhaps publishable.  This study may inform us about human acting ability and about film audiences’ ability to empathize with and make inferences about fictional characters.

If you are interested in conducting this project as your honors thesis during the AY 2007/2008, please send an application to Professor Bertram Malle, bfmalle@uoregon.edu and include the following materials:

  •             Year in school, major;
  •             psychology classes taken, with grades;
  •             film or acting classes taken (not a requirement), with grades;
  •             any other film-related or acting-related experience or interests;
  •             the name of a person who can be your reference.

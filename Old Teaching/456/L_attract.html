Attraction, Liking, Mate Selection

Major Antecedents of Liking and Attraction

  1. Proximity (or "propinquity")
    Segal (1974): Seating order (alphabetical) predicted close friendships.
    Festinger, Schachter, & Back (1950): A study of housing patterns and their effects on friendships.

    Why the effect of proximity?

    • opportunity (for meeting at all, for interaction, for further causes of liking to operate)
    • increasing familiarity ("mere exposure effect": familiarity really breeds liking, even for unknown "Chinese" characters)

  2. Physical attractiveness (PA)
    Once proximity has operated, PA is the most powerful predictor of liking.
    Walster et al. (1966): U of Minn "computer dating service." Matching hypothesis: people of comparable PA are more likely to "match up." However, the reason for this effect is not that people look for equally attractive partners . . .

    Why the effect of PA?

    • PA stereotype -- i.e., people infer positive attributes from beauty. Notes of caution: (a) People do not agree as much on ratings of PA anymore! (b) PA is a gift that can sometimes be a curse (inferences of lower intelligence; attributional ambiguity when receiving praise or benefits, harsher sentences for violent crimes). (c) Effect of PA tapers off in adult years (PA does not predict life satisfaction or relationship satisfaction).

  3. Similarity of personality, attitudes, and demographic characteristics
    Once proximity and PA have operated, this similarity is the strongest predictor of further relationship developments. People choose partners with similar age, race, class, regional origin, culture in part because of proximity (caused by societal stratification), but also because this common background makes attitude similarity more likely. Correlations between couples are .40-.60 for attitudes, .46 for education, .45 for physical attractiveness, .37 for IQ, about .15 for personality. The only feature for which complementarity holds true is the trait-pair dominance-submissiveness.

    Why the effect of similar attitudes?

    • social validation of own attitude
    • joint activities => joint experiences => self-feeding process
    • negative inferences about people who have different attitudes ("she is wrong", "he hasn't thought about the issue deeply," etc.)
    • negative affect when disagreement is touched (hard to handle in early stages of a relationship; much easier in later stages when a foundation is already built)

  4. Reciprocity of liking/Evidence of liking
    If you find out that the other person finds you attractive, your own attraction increases. Can even be a self-fulfilling prophecy (see "Much ado about nothing").

    Why this effect?

    • flattering to the ego (see Gilbert & Jones, 1986)
    • fear of rejection is lowered
    Limits: Flattery must be diagnostic (does not work when the flatterer is perceived to have an ulterior motive; does not work when the flatterer is uniformly positive).

Folk Theory of Mind and Behavior II

This session covers only two articles, but they are both complex, so detailed reading is necessary for a productive discussion .

Jones & Nisbett develop the hypothesis that actors and observers differ in how they explain behavior. In short, J & N claim that actors use more "situation causes" wherheas observers use more "person causes" (usually J & N call them "dispositions") to epxlain behavior. This hypothesis has a good deal of value, and J & N provide some evidence and plausible examples throughout the paper.

However, it gets a bit more complicated when we ask why this asymmetry should exist. J & N list a number of possible processes that might underlie the asymmetry, but until today, research has not clarified which of these processes really hold and which ones don't. One reason for this uncertainty is that J & N's original formulation might be too broad: The categories of "situation" and "disposition" are too simple to cover the complex ground of people's behavior explanations. As we discussed today, people distinguish between intentional and unintentional behaviors, and they explain them with reasons and (mere) causes, respectively. You might want to go back and read the second part of the short Malle (1997) paper to get clear about the limitations of the traditional disposition-situation dichotomy.

Malle & Knobe don't ask how actors and observers explain behavior. They explore under what conditions actors and observers wonder about and explain certain types of behaviors. In particular, M & K propose three general conditions under which people wonder why (and thus search for explanations) and they use these conditions to predict which behaviors actors and observers wonder about and explain -- in privat or communicative contexts.

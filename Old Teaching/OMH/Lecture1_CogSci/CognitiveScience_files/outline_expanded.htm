The Topic of Other Minds
Originally, a philosophical problem
More recently, a fascinating psychological phenomenon
Involving contributions from philosophy, developmental/social/cognitive psychology, anthropology, primatology, linguistics, evolutionary biology, archeology…
Basic question: How and why do humans register and reason about (other people’s) minds?

Cognitive Science
Interdisciplinary field (psychology, neuroscience, philosophy, linguistics, anthropology, artificial intelligence, evolutionary theory).
Fundamental goal: Examining the mind.
Ideal case: philosophical and theoretical questions, answered with empirical methods.
History: Cognitive revolution in the 1950s; computer metaphor; Cognitive Science Society and journal Cognitive Science were formed mid-1970s

Themes and Controversies
Cognition is representation (vs. embodied)
Cognition is computation (rules)
vs. distributed processes (no rules)
Consciousness (vs. unconscious processes)
Free will (vs. determinism, illusory choice)
Modularity, innateness, plasticity, neural localization, unique human abilities
Emotion, social cognition, self-awareness

Core Insights
Transformational grammar
Controlled vs. automatic processes
Social factors in evolution of mind (relationship between intelligence, social cognition, art, religion, mathematics, science).
Limitations of AI (frame problem)
Key role of imagination, simulation, planning
Central role of affect; perception-action link

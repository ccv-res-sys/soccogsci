Woodward
Infants were habituated to a hand reaching to and grasping one of two toys.
Test events after habituation:
“new object/old path” event:  hand reached to the old location and thus now grasped a different object.
“old object/new path” event: hand grasped the same object as in habitutation, but it was now in the other location.
If infants perceived the habituation movement in terms of the hand’s association with the target object, then they should look longer at the new object/old path event because there the hand is associated with a new object.
Indeed, 5- and 9- month infants looked longer at the new object/old path event.

Gergely et al.
Presented infants with computerized 2-dimensional displays of circles involved in intentional-like motion.
Infants were habituated to a circle moving up to and then over a wall-like barrier, then back down across the barrier to join up with a second circle.
Two test events after habituation
“direct”: barrier removed, first circle moves directly in a straight line to link up with the second circle
“indirect”: barrier removed, first circle moves in the same path as in habituation (although the barrier no longer intervened).
9- and 12-month-old infants, but not 6-month-olds, looked longer at the indirect test event.
Authors’ interpretation: infants perceived the moving circle as intentional

Phillips & Wellman
Infants see a person reach over a barrier and grasp an object.
Test events after habituation
“direct reach”: barrier is removed and person now reaches directly for the object
“indirect reach”: barrier is removed by person still reaches in a motion as if the barrier was still there.
If in habituation the infant interprets the actor’s action as object-directed, then when the barrier is removed the direct reach is the expected action and the indirect reach would be more attention-worthy.
In fact, 6- and 12-month-old infants look longer at the indirect reach.

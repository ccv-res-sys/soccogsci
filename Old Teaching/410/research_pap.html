For your empirical paper, you will collect data to back up your argument or simply find out what people think or do. For this paper, you could interview people on the street or students in your dorm; conduct a systematic observation of your own or your friends' behavior; hand out a questionnaire to your fellow students at the beginning of class; or analyze archival data such as newspapers, Congress transcripts, legal cases. Do not merely replicate a study that you read. Instead, try to find a variation on an existing method, apply the existing method to a new domain or to a new subject population, or find out whether a particular effect still holds if you change the wording of an instruction. The empirical paper might need some help from my side, so feel free to come to my office hours to talk about your ideas. Make sure you leave yourself enough time to do the empirical paper; it takes longer than you expect to collect, analyze, and write up a small data set. The empirical paper may be longer than 3 pages; but it must not exceed 6 pages (including tables, graphs, and references, but excluding an appendix with a copy of your materials).

Tips on the empirical paper

  • The purpose of the empirical paper is to practice the style of thinking that is typical for an empirical science: to collect data in order to answer a question or test a hypothesis. The study will be small, a "finger exercise," and it's okay if you don't advance the field of psychology with your results. But be clear in your question/hypothesis and try to collect some data that (partially) help you answer the question or test the hypothesis.
  • Your question/hypothesis must relate to some class topic. It can be simple and straightforward, but do not merely replicate a study from the reader. Find some variation (e.g., in the way the data are collected, conditions under which the effect might hold or not, people for which it might hold or not,...).
  • You can collect data through observation, interviews, or questionnaire, but you can also do an archival study (on magazines, newspapers, etc.). It's okay to collect data together with another student (e.g., in a joint questionnaire), but you need to be sure that you have different paper topics, and you need to acknowledge the joint collection in a footnote (otherwise the grader may get suspicious).
  • You should roughly follow APA style (an introduction, a methods section, results, and a discussion section, all with or without headings, as you please), but you don't need an abstract or a summary. You will need an appendix if you used a questionnaire or a coding scheme. You will need to add references if you refer to articles that are not in the reader.
  • The introduction should be short but clearly tell the reader what question/hypothesis you explored, how it relates to class (important!), and how you are going to try to answer it. In the methods section, describe how you collected data that might answer that question. In the results section, describe some basic data analysis. Finally, in the discussion, draw conclusions, describe how one could do a more involved study to answer the question, etc.
  • Basic data analysis means that you should tabulate your data, calculate means, proportions, or frequency counts and display them so the reader sees the results. If your data allow it, you might want to compute a simple t-test, Pearson correlation, or chi-square test, but this is not necessary. What is necessary, however, is a good display of your data (in the form of a table or graph).
  • If you collect data on people, you should have at least 10 subjects (5 in your experimental group, 5 in the control group, if applicable). If you do an archival study (analyzing newspapers, TV, etc.), the number of units observed depends very much on your question (ask me if you are unsure). The idea is that you should have enough units of observation to see tendencies in the data. Only few of you will have enough to get statistically significant results.
  • Your first draft must be complete except for the size of your sample. If you haven't been able to collect enough data by Tuesday, you can add more over Thanksgiving. But you need to hand in an otherwise complete paper.
  • Make sure that your data collection is completely without psychological and physical risk for the participants. If you think you need to use deception in your study, use only mild one, and debrief your participants thoroughly.
  • Be brief and precise in your write-up, so you don't go over 5 pages.

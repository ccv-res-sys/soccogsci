Conformity
= behavior that follows a real or imagined norm (reference group, society, culture)
Variation: imitating behavior of reference group
Conditions
1. Ambiguity (e.g., pitted windshields)
2. Others perceived as experts (e.g., group think, Jonestown)
3. Stress (e.g., Invasion from Mars)
4. Need for affiliation (e.g., Asch situation)

The Asch Situation

2/3 “critical trials”;
Variations:
Writing down answers

Asch: Group size 1 < 2 < 3 ≈ 4 and above
Recent research: monotonously linear

The Milgram Experiment
Set: “Experiment on punishment & memory”;
teacher & learner; paid in advance;
shock levels with each error;
exper.’s responses to
protests are scripted
Results: 65% went
all the way;
100% went > 300V

Experimenter “Communication”
"Please continue" or "Please go on."
"The experiment requires that you continue."
"It is absolutely essential that you continue."
"You have no other choice, you must go on."

Determinants of Obedience
Gradual increase of shock intensity with no cut-off point; if you administered x V, there is no reason to stop at x + 15V
No apparent "exit channel" (except 450V mark!)
Diffusion of responsibility (righteous experimenter)
Experimenter communication is odd and non-cooperative
Subjects glide into a weird, confusing situation (cf. Asch experiment) in which no rational behavior is apparent
Massive tension and dissonance, but no way to reduce it
Subjects' actions and cognitions are dissociated (e.g.,
saying "I can't go on," but nevertheless going on)

Successful Reductions
Proximity to victim reduces to 30% (touching)
Distance from experimenter (phone) reduces to 23%
Defiant other (at 210V) reduces to 10%
Free voltage choice reduces to 5% (most < 150V)
New teacher reduces to 20% (0% if initial teacher becomes learner and protests)
Contradictory experimenters reduces to 0%

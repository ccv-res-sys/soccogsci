11/5: Satellites of the Self

'Satellites' of the self are people, objects, teams, etc. that fulfill functions that normally only the self itself can fulfill (e.g., to increase self-regard, one might be a fan of a sports team; to remember certain things, one may rely on one's partner).

Even though having different satellites or 'characters' to oneself can be helpful and enriching, it may disturb the integratedness of the self, which is very important. Discussion about whether a person can have several characters and still an integrated self, and how integrated does it need to be, was lively and raised many questions about how one can or should deal with all different satellites of the self.

People are somewhat inconsistent and have to balance between all the external influences and expectations they face. People make assumptions about others and compartmentalize them according to their own preferences and usually aren't able or willing to see all the "selves"; it is often hard to convince people that one is more than the particular self THEY see. External cues promote certain aspects and specific selves, but there's also pressure to be consistent. The question of how biased one is to conform or identify with certain people, to emphasize a certain part of one's persona was discussed. There was agreement that external cues can completely move a personon from one self to another, but they cannot make the person forget about the other'selves. People occasionaly distance themselves from some selves.

There can be contradictory parts to oneself, too. A particular situation, for example, can change people's views of themselves. There can be discrepancies without the feeling of inconsistency. It is okay to have different and contradicting selves as long as they are integrated harmoniously. There is pressure to keep a unified idea of oneself and any threat of that unity creates conflict. Pennebaker writes about the idea of integration - about recapturing the satellite, bringing the satellite back.

There was quite a lot of discussion about Pennebaker's studies in which people had to revisit their traumatic experiences, and the validity of that test was criticized. Bertram brought up the fact that, unfortunately, the psychological processes of this "successful therapy" were not analyzed.

Networks of representations of self are connections, links to other things; integration can come from a continuous chain of associations within this network. One needs to create these links or pathways for unconnected, especially traumatic things. It is necessary for one's unity and integration to place these experiences and create networks instead of letting them 'float around freely', pop up unexpectedly and disturb the person. "Dissociation" may be the strongest form of "cutting links" and compartmentalizing certain experiences.

There was a long discussion on 'internet-selves' and the pros and cons of living as a different persona in the internet. Basic good aspects were that it can be theraupetic and people can work through problems on an experimental level before they apply it to real life, but the negative aspect of this is that people may loose touch with reality and may not be able to apply what they have learned or experienced in the internet to real life.

How to integrate negative satellites of the self? What is the true self of a person? Does one have to supress the negative satellites and how does this affect integration and unity? The main question of all satellites is: how does one balance them and have a harmony between them?

In a discussion about affiliation with sports teams and music groups, an argument was brought up that one is very unlikely to change affiliation to a sports team whereas it is quite likely when it comes to music groups, individual athletes, or social partners. Teams serve as a continued identity or a satellite. What exactly happens when the team is not doing well is unclear. Fans do not seem to happily switch to a more successful team. Why? Intermittent reinformcement? The norm of loyality? A special (evolved?) need to affiliate with competitive groups? Gender differences of affiliations were also disputed.

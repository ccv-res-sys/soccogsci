10/7: Actor-observer asymmetries

We discussed

  1. Miscommunication between actor/observer or self/other
    • the role of privileged information (asymmetry in accessibility of information: experience of actor vs. knowledge of the observer)
    • actor's overestimate the availability of one's internal experiences to observers (hypothesis: partly because actors rely on their own experience and intention)
  2. Communicative Experiences
    • actors rely on feedback by others to judge what they have communicated; actors must read the reactions of others
      • gap between what we think we have communicated and the feedback given by others concerning that information
    • actors must express, detect the impact of their expression (redundancy of cues)
    • this communication is not limited to voluntary responses; this could be oversimplistic
    • actor's emotional expressiveness is usually perceived by the actor as more intense than what they actually expressed (hyp: influenced by nonverbal cues, gender, culture, individual differences)
  3. The Communication Gap
    • the gap found in everyday life is less than that of the gap found in the lab setting (how could we successfully interact if this was not the case?)
    • as communication increases, gap closes
    • problems inferring from the lab setting to everyday experiences
  4. The Experimental Situation
    • what is the standard that we use to evaluate vignettes (just the content of the vignette or also the context of it being an experiment, the experimenter "communicating" with us?)
    • expectations concerning subject's cognitive functioning (when will they be confused?)
    • what is the normative (correct) answer?
    • subjects take the knowledge they have, project it, and thereby infer intentions
  5. Effects of Communication and Social Interaction
    • increased social interaction causes the actor/observer phenomenon to disappear
  6. Are experiments slices of reality, or are they simply artificial situations?
    • does reality dictate the phenomenon to be studied, or does the phenomenon under study suggest what reality is like?
    • problem of "back-translating" research findings to the everyday phenomena that they were inspired by (or which they were supposed to explain)
